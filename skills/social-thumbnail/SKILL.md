# Social Thumbnail

Create a clear visual promise that the content really keeps. The image must stay easy to understand at the size and crop where people see it. This skill explains design and export decisions without bundled converters, scripts, parser data or automatic updates.

## Brief and content evidence

Collect the real content, audience, platform/surface, language, exact text, brand/style references, aspect ratio, pixel dimensions, upload device, required format and byte limit. Ask only about important choices that are still open. Use 16:9 by default when the surface is not named, and say that you assumed it.

Find the moment, result, question or subject the content really supports. A title can give context while the thumbnail gives visual evidence or curiosity. Do not suggest an endorsement, fake a before/after, make up a feature or exaggerate a result the content never shows.

Use [the brief template](assets/thumbnail-brief.md) to write down the concept, focal subject, intended reading order, text, crop limits, export target and the question you will judge it by, before you generate artwork.

## Build the visual order on purpose

Choose one main focal subject and a clear path through subject, headline and supporting detail. Group related elements close together, align them consistently, reuse a small set of visual elements and create meaningful contrast. Robin Williams's [The Non-Designer's Design Book, fourth edition](https://www.peachpit.com/store/non-designers-design-book-9780133966152) is professional further reading on those design principles, checked through the publisher record.

A short headline of about two to six words can be a useful starting point, but language and purpose decide the final choice. If the viewer needs a paragraph to understand the image, rework the concept before you shrink the text.

Choose type for the real display size and the content's tone. Avoid thin/ornate/condensed faces when they become hard to read. Check accents, names, symbols, line breaks and letter spacing. Use a clear separation treatment where text would otherwise get lost in a busy background. Decorative shadows and outlines should make things clearer, not add clutter.

Use color that fits the theme and a focal point that stands out. Check grayscale to find hierarchy that depends only on hue. Extreme saturation, red arrows or exaggerated faces are genre choices, not universal marketing rules. [Practical Typography](https://practicaltypography.com/summary-of-key-rules.html) is useful further reading on type decisions, but do not copy its print-body advice straight into thumbnail sizing.

## Compose for the real placement

Work with the whole frame, then check likely feed/grid/player crops and interface overlays. Keep key text/faces/results away from cutoffs and duration badges. A generic five-percent inset can start a composition, but it cannot replace the real surface's crop.

A square, landscape and portrait design need different layouts. Do not squeeze a landscape subject or line of type into 9:16. Rearrange, crop on purpose or extend the supporting background. Keep heads, hands, products, screens, circles and typography in correct proportion.

Recognition should not depend on remembering an unexplained symbol. The [Nielsen Norman Group discussion of recognition and recall](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/recognition-and-recall/) gives relevant usability reasoning. Its interface findings are not a measured promise of thumbnail CTR.

## Create and inspect imagery

Use the available image model for requested original artwork or image edits. A generated raster master is useful for illustration, while exact logos and brand typography may need editable vector/text layout. Respect rights and keep a record of where real sources came from.

Check generated anatomy, geometry, perspective, object count, lettering, face identity where it matters, shadows and how objects relate physically. Do not call a result professional just because it is high resolution. Fix obvious defects before you make variants.

Use real product captures when you show software. Decorative AI artwork must not become fake UI evidence. Add exact text with a suitable editable typography tool when you cannot trust the model's glyphs or alignment.

The finished image must read as natural content for its audience. Do not put production notes, prompt text, model or tool names, "AI generated" captions or agent references in the artwork unless the user asks. When a platform policy requires an AI disclosure, it goes in that platform's own setting, such as YouTube Studio's "AI use" attribute for realistic altered or synthetic video content. YouTube's disclosure guidance treats AI help with a thumbnail as production help that does not by itself require that disclosure. Keep the brief, prompts and tool notes in the delivery report, not in the image.

## Platform and export profile

Research baseline: 2026-11-05. Stored limits change only when the user asks for an update. Check the real target/interface when you produce a deliverable, and state any uncertainty without quietly rewriting this skill.

| Surface | Checked canvas guidance | Important boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| YouTube video | Current official recommendation 3840×2160, 16:9, minimum width 640, JPG or PNG | The thumbnail file limit is 2 MB when uploaded from mobile and 50 MB from desktop Studio. Custom thumbnails need a verified account |
| YouTube Shorts | Vertical 9:16, current guidance 2160×3840, minimum height 640 | Custom upload is documented only in desktop Studio for verified accounts |
| Podcast playlist | Square 1:1 | 10 MB mobile and 50 MB desktop limits |
| Instagram/TikTok vertical cover | A 1080×1920 layout is a useful starting canvas | Grid/player/feed crops and native selection support differ |
| X/LinkedIn media | The real post/video/link-preview placement | These are separate products with separate limits |

The owner's portable preset is a large PNG master and a 16:9 JPEG copy starting at quality 99 under 2,000,000 bytes. This is a cautious chosen delivery profile, not a universal platform rule. Quality 99 is an encoder setting, not “99 percent identical,” and it does not promise that a 4K image fits.

Use the available editor/export interface to choose dimensions, fit/crop, quality and format on purpose. Never quietly lower quality, crop away evidence, stretch artwork or replace the master after a size failure. If a chosen export goes over the limit, explain the real tradeoff and use an adjustment that is already approved, or ask for the missing decision.

Apply the real orientation metadata. Convert known color profiles properly for normal sRGB delivery, instead of stripping metadata while leaving pixel values that do not match. Flatten transparency onto a chosen background for JPEG. Do not upscale a small source and claim it has new captured detail. See [the export procedure](references/export.md) and [platform detail](references/platforms.md).

## Worked storyboard and critique

For a real tutorial about organizing reading notes, one concept can show a real scattered-versus-organized result with a short benefit headline. A second can focus on the successful search result and a more specific question. Both must match what the content shows.

Check whether the difference is visible at about 320px wide. If both sides shrink into unreadable tiny cards, enlarge the real evidence, cut supporting objects or choose another concept. Making the headline bigger alone will not save an unclear visual story.

Check the real exported file at full size and at storefront/feed size. Check every glyph, subject proportion, crop, overlap, alignment and UI overlay. Confirm that no prompt fragment, tool watermark or production note appears in the image. Reopen it and check the real format, dimensions and byte size through the file/editor properties you have. A filename extension does not prove the encoding.

## Evaluate variants and deliver

When variants are requested, change a meaningful concept, such as subject framing or the benefit shown. Do not present three nearly identical colors as separate creative ideas to test. Define the expected audience response and the metric that matters before testing.

Current eligible YouTube tools can test up to three title/thumbnail combinations and judge them by watch-time performance. Check that this is really available. Pair click-through with retention/satisfaction and compare equal audiences. Uploads at different times, one after another, do not prove that a change caused an improvement.

Deliver the master, requested copies, editable text/layout where available, the brief and the real export/check results. Preparing an image is not permission to publish it or run platform experiments. [Official thumbnail guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72431?hl=en), [creator packaging guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300?hl=en) and [source notes](references/sources.md) separate platform rules from these editorial recommendations.
