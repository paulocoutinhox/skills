# Design and marketing

## Message and hierarchy

A thumbnail and title should make a coherent promise that the content fulfills. Show one clear story, result, contrast, or question. Do not fabricate a before/after, imply a person endorsed something, or exaggerate an outcome unsupported by the content. Emotional expression can work when it matches the genre and audience. It is not mandatory.

Use a strong focal subject and a deliberate reading order: subject, main text, supporting detail. Keep the number of competing elements small. A short headline of roughly two to six words is a useful starting point, not a platform rule. The final choice depends on language, audience, subject, and display size.

Choose typography for actual legibility. Avoid very thin, ornate, overly condensed, or tightly spaced text when it fails at thumbnail size. Use theme-appropriate display type, adequate letter spacing, clean alignment, and enough contrast. Keep diacritics, punctuation, names, and technical terms exact. Do not put text over visually busy areas without a clear separation treatment.

Choose colors that fit the content and brand while making the focal point distinct. Test grayscale and small-scale readability. Use restrained outlines or shadows when they improve separation. Do not treat extreme saturation, red arrows, shocked faces, or a particular color as a universal marketing rule.

## Composition and proportionality

Use balanced spacing, deliberate framing, and a useful subject scale. Check heads, hands, products, perspective, circles, screens, and typography for distortions. Keep faces and important text away from crop boundaries. Inspect thumbnails with likely duration badges and interface overlays. A 5% inset can be an initial design margin, but exact platform crop and safe areas must be verified for the surface.

Do not force a 16:9 design into 9:16 by squeezing it. Create a separate composition or use an approved crop. Generate or extend additional background when needed. Cropping must preserve the story and all important text. For grid previews and vertical-video covers, review all relevant crops, not just the full cover canvas.

## Evaluation

Inspect the actual exported artifact at full size, mobile scale, and alongside representative feed examples. Confirm the message can be understood quickly without reading the description. Verify that the title and thumbnail complement rather than contradict one another.

For a supported A/B test, define a hypothesis and use meaningful variants. Current eligible YouTube surfaces can compare up to three title/thumbnail combinations and select by watch-time performance. Availability and restrictions must be checked. Do not declare a winner from unmatched sequential time periods or from click-through alone when retention falls. No visual formula guarantees performance.
