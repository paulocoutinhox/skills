# Design and marketing

## Message and hierarchy

A thumbnail and title should make one consistent promise that the content keeps. Show one clear story, result, contrast or question. Do not fake a before/after, suggest a person endorsed something or exaggerate an outcome the content does not support. Emotional expression can work when it fits the genre and audience. It is not required.

Use a strong focal subject and a planned reading order: subject, main text, supporting detail. Keep the number of competing elements small. A short headline of about two to six words is a useful starting point, not a platform rule. The final choice depends on language, audience, subject and display size.

Choose typography that people can really read. Avoid very thin, ornate, overly condensed or tightly spaced text when it fails at thumbnail size. Use display type that fits the theme, enough letter spacing, clean alignment and enough contrast. Keep accents, punctuation, names and technical terms exact. Do not put text over busy areas without a clear separation treatment.

Choose colors that fit the content and brand while making the focal point stand out. Test grayscale and readability at small size. Use light outlines or shadows when they improve separation. Do not treat extreme saturation, red arrows, shocked faces or one particular color as a universal marketing rule.

## Composition and proportionality

Use balanced spacing, planned framing and a useful subject size. Check heads, hands, products, perspective, circles, screens and typography for distortion. Keep faces and important text away from crop edges. Check thumbnails with likely duration badges and interface overlays. A 5% inset can be a first design margin, but you must check the exact platform crop and safe areas for the surface.

Do not force a 16:9 design into 9:16 by squeezing it. Create a separate layout or use an approved crop. Generate or extend extra background when needed. Cropping must keep the story and all important text. For grid previews and vertical-video covers, check every crop that applies, not just the full cover canvas.

## Evaluation

Check the real exported file at full size, at mobile size and next to typical feed examples. The image should read as natural content, with no prompt text, tool or model names, production notes or AI captions unless the user asked for them. A platform-required AI disclosure goes in the platform's disclosure setting, not in the artwork. Confirm that people can understand the message quickly without reading the description. Check that the title and thumbnail support each other and do not contradict each other.

For a supported A/B test, define a hypothesis and use meaningful variants. Current eligible YouTube surfaces can compare up to three title/thumbnail combinations and pick one by watch-time performance. You must check availability and restrictions. Do not declare a winner from unmatched time periods one after another, or from click-through alone when retention drops. No visual formula promises performance.
