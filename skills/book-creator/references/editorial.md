# Editorial styles and ABNT-based defaults

## Select the correct scope

Academic presentation and commercial book design are different settings. The default is an ABNT-based editorial profile, with configurable book typography and a white interior. For strict academic compliance, read the current standard and the institution's guide. Do not call a custom small-trim book, a centered page number, or a decorative font “strict ABNT” just because it uses author-date citations.

Verified edition base: NBR 14724:2024, corrected in 2025, for academic presentation. NBR 10520:2023 for citations. NBR 6023:2025 for references. Also check NBR 6024 where it applies for section numbering, NBR 6027 for contents, NBR 6028 for abstracts, and NBR 6029 for books. Check later amendments before you claim compliance. This guide sums up working decisions and does not copy the standards.

## Default style sheet

| Element | ABNT-based editorial preset | Classification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Workbook page | A4, 210 × 297 mm, portrait. | Owner default. |
| Body | Arial, 12 pt, black on white, 1.5 line spacing. | Arial is an owner choice. ABNT does not require this specific font family in all cases. |
| Recto margins | Top and inner/left 30 mm, bottom and outer/right 20 mm. | Academic base, subject to the verified institutional scope. |
| Duplex margins | Mirror inner/outer margins on verso pages. Increase the gutter if the printer needs more room. | Production must fit the chosen binding. |
| Paragraph | First-line indent 12.5 mm, 0 pt extra between ordinary paragraphs, justified to both margins with the last line flush left. | Justification is a required owner rule. The indent is an owner editorial choice, not a universal ABNT measurement. |
| Heading 1 | 14 pt bold, clear hierarchy, new chapter on a new page where chosen. | Editorial choice. |
| Heading 2 and 3 | 12 pt bold and 12 pt regular, consistent spacing and numbering. | Editorial choice. |
| Short quotation | Up to three lines, inline with quotation marks and a precise citation. | Citation base. |
| Long quotation | More than three lines, block treatment without quotation marks, smaller type and single spacing. Use 40 mm left indent as the chosen recommended preset. | Check the current standard and institutional requirements. |
| Notes and sources | 10 pt, single spacing, justified footnotes, linked note markers, consistent sequence. | Smaller size chosen by the editorial profile. |
| Figure | Label, number, and chosen title above. Source or credit below. Keep as one logical block. | Caption/title and source are separate. |
| Tables | Number and title above, source and needed notes below, repeated headers across pages. | Follow the chosen table standard and avoid unreadable scaling. |
| References | Consistent entry style, single spacing, left alignment, visible space between entries. | Check the NBR 6023 edition and the institution's guide. |
| Interior background | White/unpainted, including blank pages. No full-page tinted fill. | Required owner production rule. |

Do not flatten all text into a single body style. Define separate styles for headings, inline emphasis, strong emphasis, foreign terms, direct quotes, block quotes, captions, source notes, footnotes, numbered lists, bullets, tables, equations, code, bibliographic entries, and cross-references. Code and equations may need other fitting fonts. Keep their backgrounds unpainted in print output.

## Citations, footnotes, and bibliography

Default to author-date citations with a bibliography. Keep source notes or explanatory footnotes where useful. If the user chooses numeric citation notes, use that system the same way throughout and confirm its bibliography requirements. Do not mix systems at random or repeat a full reference in every footnote.

Write author names with the capitalization the current citation style requires. Do not uppercase every name in parentheses by habit. Direct quotations need a page or other precise locator when one exists. A paraphrase must match what its source says. Label translations, omissions, or emphasis as the chosen style requires. Handle corporate authors, missing authors, multiple authors, same-author/same-year works, indirect citations, and online media through the current standard instead of inventing fields.

The bibliography must match the sources that are really cited. Collect author or organization, title and subtitle, edition, publication place where applicable, publisher, year, volume/issue/pages, DOI or stable URL, and access date where applicable. Handle books, chapters, articles, web pages, reports, legislation, theses, datasets, software, and audiovisual material with their type-specific fields. Do not make up missing metadata. Prefer original sources over secondhand mentions.

An automatic CSL or bibliography style may follow an older edition of the standard. Check its documented version and rendered examples before you call it compliant with NBR 6023:2025. A typesetting engine does not check reference quality by itself.

## Book anatomy and order

Build the book only from the usual parts that help its readers. Ask which optional parts the author wants, and write each one with real content. Leave out an element instead of filling it with generic or placeholder text, and leave out a list that would be empty.

| Part | Purpose | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cover | Title, subtitle, author, cover art. | Designed separately from the interior. |
| Half-title | Title only, on a recto. | Common in commercial books. |
| Title page | Full title, subtitle, author, publisher or city and year. | Required. Exact metadata must match the cover. |
| Copyright page | Copyright notice, edition, ISBN, real cataloging data and permissions. | Verso of the title page. Never invent ISBNs or cataloging records. |
| Dedication | A short personal dedication from the author. | Optional. Ask the author for the wording. |
| Epigraph | A short quotation with its source. | Optional. The quotation needs a verified source and rights. |
| Acknowledgments | The author's thanks. | Optional. Written from the author's perspective about the work's subject, never about tools. |
| Contents | Generated from real headings. | Called "Sumário" in Portuguese ABNT mode. |
| List of figures and tables | Generated from captions. | ABNT calls the figure list "Lista de ilustrações". Include only when chosen and not empty. |
| Abbreviations and symbols | Terms used in the text. | Optional. |
| Foreword, preface or introduction | Presents the work's purpose, scope and audience. | A foreword is written by someone other than the author. |
| Chapters | The body of the work. | Numbered by the chosen style. |
| Conclusion | Final considerations about the subject. | Optional in commercial books. |
| Appendices and annexes | Supporting material by the author and by others respectively. | Keep the two apart in ABNT mode. |
| Glossary | Defined terms. | Optional. |
| References | Every cited source and nothing else. | Titled "Referências" in Portuguese ABNT mode. |
| Index | Hand-picked subject terms with page locators. | Optional and generated only after final pagination. |
| About the author | Short biography supplied or approved by the author. | Optional. |

Commercial books commonly follow the order half-title, title page, copyright page, dedication, epigraph, contents, lists, foreword, preface, acknowledgments and introduction. In strict ABNT academic mode, the contents is the last pre-textual element, after the dedication, acknowledgments, epigraph, abstracts and lists. The ABNT-based editorial default uses this ABNT pre-textual order. Follow the chosen style's order, and keep it the same in the contents, the bookmarks and the physical page order.

Write the preface, introduction, acknowledgments and author biography about the work and its subject. None of these parts describes how the manuscript, illustrations or files were produced.

## Front matter and pagination

Choose the elements that fit the publication. Do not put an academic approval sheet or abstract into every commercial book. Copyright information and an ISBN must be real. The model must not invent a professional cataloging record.

Academic mode counts pages by the chosen standard and shows numbers starting at the right textual section. Check how the title page, cataloging page, and duplex versos are handled. Strict academic recto/verso placement can differ from the pagination the user picks freely for a book. For editorial books, write down the chosen counting and display policy clearly. Hide running heads on chosen opening or blank pages without breaking the count.
