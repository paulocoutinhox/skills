# Marketing workflow

## Strategy before wording

Identify the audience's immediate problem or desire, its knowledge level, and the desired action. Choose awareness, qualified viewing, saves, conversation, leads, or conversion as the primary goal. Pick a message supported by the actual content. Avoid generic “amazing,” “game-changing,” or “you won't believe” claims without substance.

Research the audience's vocabulary and search intent using available first-party analytics and relevant current examples. Use official creator lessons and credible practitioners as evidence for hypotheses, not as a universal algorithm formula. A book or framework can supply ideas, but do not claim to have consulted an inaccessible book or video transcript.

## Writing decisions

- Lead with a specific useful promise, concrete result, question, or unresolved problem the content actually answers.
- Front-load meaning because previews truncate differently across devices. There is no fixed universal “visible first line” character count.
- Support a claim with an example, demonstration, source, or accurate detail. Never invent numbers or quote unseen material.
- Use natural keywords in titles and descriptions. Avoid keyword stuffing, walls of hashtags, repetitive slogans, and irrelevant mentions.
- Prefer one primary action, such as watching the full demonstration, saving a checklist, reading a relevant page, or answering a substantive question.
- Use line breaks for readable groups. Keep emoji deliberate and compatible with the brand and audience.
- Include disclosures, sponsorship information, and factual limitations where they apply. Do not hide required information after unnecessary filler.
- Match language, spelling, units, examples, and timezone to the intended audience. The skill instructions are English, but requested social copy follows the user's chosen language.

## Platform adaptation

YouTube packaging should align title, thumbnail, opening, and content. A thumbnail can add visual curiosity while the title supplies specific context. Descriptions should identify the subject before channel boilerplate. Tags support identification and spelling variants rather than replacing useful copy.

Short-form copy should make sense alongside the first visible or spoken moment. Supply a hook the actual edit can deliver. Do not copy performance-ad guidance into an organic post as if the same outcome were guaranteed. TikTok's creator guidance favors an early hook and clear action, but audience retention must validate the specific execution.

Instagram copy should support the visual, provide context, and make saving or responding worthwhile. LinkedIn copy should respect professional attention, show concrete expertise, and avoid contrived engagement prompts. X copy should make one idea understandable without requiring a chain of unexplained references.

## Measurement

Propose a hypothesis and a primary metric before testing. Compare like audiences and distribution surfaces. For video packaging, consider click-through and watch time or retention together. For posts, consider meaningful interactions, saves, qualified traffic, or conversion according to the goal. Do not declare a winner from a tiny sample or a single hour of unrelated distribution.

When creating variants, change a meaningful dimension such as searchable versus curiosity-led framing or a different audience benefit. Preserve truth and the offer. Provide two or three useful alternatives when requested, not dozens of superficial rewrites. A high click rate paired with poor satisfaction can signal a misleading promise. No posting schedule, hashtag count, or formula guarantees virality.
