# Marketing workflow

## Strategy before wording

Find the audience's immediate problem or wish, how much they already know, and the action you want. Choose awareness, qualified viewing, saves, conversation, leads or conversion as the main goal. Pick a message the real content supports. Avoid empty “amazing,” “game-changing” or “you won't believe” claims.

Research the audience's words and search intent with the first-party analytics you have and relevant current examples. Use official creator lessons and credible practitioners as evidence for ideas to test, not as a universal algorithm formula. A book or framework can give ideas, but do not claim you read a book or video transcript you could not access.

## Writing decisions

- Open with a specific useful promise, concrete result, question or open problem that the content really answers.
- Put the meaning first, because previews cut text off differently on different devices. There is no fixed “visible first line” character count.
- Back up a claim with an example, demonstration, source or accurate detail. Never make up numbers or quote material you have not seen.
- Use natural keywords in titles and descriptions. Avoid keyword stuffing, walls of hashtags, repeated slogans and irrelevant mentions.
- Prefer one main action, such as watching the full demonstration, saving a checklist, reading a relevant page or answering a real question.
- Use line breaks to make readable groups. Use emoji on purpose, in a way that fits the brand and audience.
- Include disclosures, sponsorship information and factual limits where they apply. Do not bury required information after needless filler.
- Match language, spelling, units, examples and time zone to the intended audience. The skill instructions are in English, but the social copy uses the language the user chose.

## Platform adaptation

YouTube packaging should line up the title, thumbnail, opening and content. A thumbnail can add visual curiosity while the title gives specific context. Descriptions should name the subject before channel boilerplate. Tags help with identification and spelling variants. They do not replace useful copy.

Short-form copy should make sense next to the first visible or spoken moment. Give a hook the real edit can deliver. Do not copy performance-ad advice into an organic post as if it promised the same outcome. TikTok's creator guidance favors an early hook and a clear action, but audience retention must confirm that the specific post works.

Instagram copy should support the visual, give context and make saving or replying worthwhile. LinkedIn copy should respect professional attention, show concrete expertise and avoid forced engagement prompts. X copy should make one idea clear without needing a chain of unexplained references.

## Measurement

Suggest a hypothesis and a main metric before testing. Compare similar audiences and distribution surfaces. For video packaging, look at click-through and watch time or retention together. For posts, look at meaningful interactions, saves, qualified traffic or conversion, depending on the goal. Do not declare a winner from a tiny sample or a single hour of unrelated distribution.

When you make variants, change a meaningful dimension, such as search-friendly versus curiosity-led framing, or a different audience benefit. Keep the truth and the offer the same. Give two or three useful options when asked, not dozens of shallow rewrites. A high click rate with low satisfaction can mean the promise was misleading. No posting schedule, hashtag count or formula promises virality.
