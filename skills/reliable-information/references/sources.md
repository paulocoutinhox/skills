# Research-method sources

Verified on 2026-11-05. These sources cover specific search and status checks, not the truth of
every future answer. Each task still needs sources relevant to its real topic.

- [Google Scholar search help](https://scholar.google.com/intl/en/scholar/help.html): finding work,
  title and author searches, citations, related articles, access, and coverage limits.
- [Google Scholar overview](https://scholar.google.com/intl/en/scholar/about.html): many kinds of
  scholarly material, which explains why being indexed is not a peer-review or quality guarantee.
- [Crossmark](https://www.crossref.org/services/crossmark/): corrections, retractions, and updates
  reported by publishers. Gaps in who takes part and in metadata coverage mean a missing notice
  proves little.
- [Crossref Retraction Watch](https://www.crossref.org/documentation/retrieve-metadata/retraction-watch/):
  another way to check status, with different coverage for retractions and other notices. Checked
  2026-11-18: the page documents the database Crossref acquired in September 2023, notes that
  expressions of concern and corrections are less complete than retractions, and offers the
  records through the REST API `update-to` field and a public CSV dataset.
- [COPE retraction guidelines](https://doi.org/10.24318/cope.2019.1.4): ethical status and linked
  publication notices. Apply the current version and respect the document's license.

No book or out-of-reach standard is claimed as fully read. Source count, publisher brand, citation
count, and the presence of a DOI do not on their own certify a factual answer. No automatic research
service or independent model benchmark was created or run during authoring.
