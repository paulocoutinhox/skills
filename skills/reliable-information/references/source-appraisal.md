# Source appraisal decisions

## Fit, independence, and accessibility

Write the claim in one sentence and name the evidence needed to support it. A standard can show an
interface requirement. A controlled comparison can estimate an effect under the conditions studied.
A professional book can explain a decision framework. These sources play different roles as
evidence. Do not use an impressive source to support a claim it never tested.

Find where the evidence first came from. Ten summaries of one survey are not ten replications. Keep
the reporting, the quoted institution's statement, and the underlying document apart. Check the real
disclosures, corrections, publication status, and the author's relevant expertise. A formal
qualification does not make every statement well supported. A commercial tie is a possible conflict
to examine, not automatic proof that a claim is false.

## Scientific material

Confirm the design and whether it fits the question. Check the question, population, comparison
group, intervention or exposure, outcome definitions, sampling, uncertainty, confounders, missing
data, and limitations. Tell preregistered analyses (planned in advance) apart from exploratory
findings where that difference changes how to read them. Read corrections and linked notices instead
of quietly citing the original version. For a review, look at how evidence was chosen and judged.
The word systematic is not enough on its own.

## Traceability and stopping

Keep short notes that tie important claims to the passages, versions, and dates you read. Do not
collect private copies of sources or create a research database nobody asked for. If access is
limited, state the limit and look for legal alternative versions. Stop with a clearly stated
uncertainty when no more reliable evidence is available. Never fill the gap with made-up confidence.

Stop searching when one of these conditions holds, and record which one applied:

- An original source that governs the claim, such as the specification, statute or vendor contract
  that applies, answers the claim directly for the stated version, jurisdiction and date.
- Independent sources with suitable methods agree, and further results repeat the same origin.
- The relevant databases and original records have been searched with the claim's terms, synonyms
  and citation trails without finding support. Report that scope and do not make the claim.
